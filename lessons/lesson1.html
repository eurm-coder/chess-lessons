Lesson 1: The Chessboard, Pieces, and Piece Values

Level: Beginner • About 10 minutes • Lesson 1

Objectives

  • Identify the eight files, eight ranks, and the coordinates of a chessboard.
  • Understand the relative values of the chess pieces.
  • Use piece values to make better decisions about exchanges.

The chessboard

A chessboard has 64 squares arranged in eight rows and eight columns. The vertical columns are called files and are labelled a through h. The horizontal rows are called ranks and are numbered 1 through 8.

Every square has a coordinate. For example, the white king begins on e1, while the black king begins on e8. Learning square names will help you read chess notation and follow lessons more easily.

Board setup rule: When you sit down to play, make sure there is always a light-coloured square in the bottom-right corner.

Piece values

Chess pieces have approximate values. These are not exact scores, but they are useful for deciding whether an exchange is usually favorable.

Piece Approximate value
Pawn 1 point
Knight 3 points
Bishop 3 points
Rook 5 points
Queen 9 points
King Invaluable — losing the king loses the game.

For example, trading a rook worth about 5 points for a knight worth about 3 points is usually not a good exchange. However, context matters: a tactical checkmate, a passed pawn, or an attack on the king can be more important than material.
